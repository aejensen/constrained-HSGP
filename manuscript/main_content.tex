\begin{abstract}
We develop a common construction for imposing positivity, monotonicity,
or convexity throughout an interval in Hilbert space Gaussian process
regression. The model represents the process as a finite sum of basis
functions with Gaussian coefficients whose variances are given by the
kernel's spectral density. We retain this representation and express
each restriction as a threshold on one coefficient. Integrating out
this coefficient gives a strongly log-concave marginal posterior with
a normal survival weight, and conditional truncated normal sampling
recovers the full constrained law. We characterize the thresholds
through polynomial equations and bound posterior error from conservative
numerical evaluation in total variation. Under spectral regularity,
these posteriors converge in quadratic Wasserstein distance to the
corresponding underlying posteriors as the computational interval
expands and higher frequencies are retained, controlling uniform
discrepancies in the response and derivatives through the constrained
order. Mercury lidar and leaf light-response applications illustrate
the consequences for derivative inference.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textit{Keywords:} Bayesian regression, continuum constraint,
derivative inference, Gaussian process, Hilbert space approximation

\section{Applications}\label{sec:applications}
We compare ordinary Gaussian and shape-constrained posteriors in two
applications where derivatives have direct scientific interpretations.
Monotonicity ensures nonnegative mercury concentration in a lidar model,
while concavity represents a nonincreasing marginal gain in leaf carbon
assimilation.
